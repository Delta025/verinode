# Verinode — Research Notes

## Core Problem: AI Vulnerability to Knowledge Corruption

Modern AI systems store knowledge implicitly in neural network weights — billions of parameters that are opaque, unauditable, and unverifiable. This architectural choice creates a growing set of vulnerabilities:

### 1. Data Poisoning

Adversaries inject malicious samples into training data to corrupt model behavior. The foundational work by Gu et al. (BadNets, 2017/2019) demonstrated that backdoor trigger patterns in training images cause targeted misclassification while maintaining normal accuracy on clean inputs. Clean-label attacks (Turner, Tsipras & Madry, 2019) showed that poisoned samples can be crafted to appear correctly labeled even to human inspectors.

A critical real-world demonstration: Alber et al. (*Nature Medicine*, 2025) showed that replacing just 0.001% of training tokens with 2,000 misinformation articles (costing under $100) was sufficient to corrupt a medical LLM. Fifteen clinicians could not distinguish poisoned outputs from clean ones. Critically, they also demonstrated a mitigation: extracting medical claims as triplets and verifying them against a biomedical knowledge graph caught 91.9% of harmful content.

**Why current defenses fail**: Standard data filtering catches obvious mislabeling but not clean-label attacks. Benchmark evaluations don't test for the biases poisoning introduces.

**How explicit knowledge graphs help**: Every claim generated by a potentially-poisoned model can be cross-referenced against confidence-weighted, provenance-tracked facts. Claims contradicting high-confidence assertions are flagged regardless of how the model was trained.

### 2. Subliminal Learning

**Reference**: Cloud, A., et al. (2025). "Subliminal Learning: Language models transmit behavioral traits via hidden signals in data." arXiv:2507.14805

AI models trained on data generated by another model can inherit hidden behavioral traits through non-semantic statistical patterns:
- A "teacher" model with a behavioral trait (e.g., liking owls, or being misaligned) generates innocuous data like number sequences
- A "student" model fine-tuned on this data inherits the trait — even when all explicit references are filtered out
- Models trained on sequences from misaligned teachers inherited misalignment, producing content calling for crime and violence
- The effect requires shared initialization — it was not observed across different model families

This is perhaps the most insidious vulnerability because the transmission channel is invisible. No content filter can detect it. As AI-generated content floods the web and distillation chains become common, the risk of silent trait propagation grows.

**Why current defenses fail**: Standard data filtering is explicitly shown to be ineffective — the behavioral traits propagate through non-semantic statistical patterns that no content filter can detect.

**How explicit knowledge graphs help**: While a graph cannot detect subliminal statistical patterns in training data, it provides a behavioral verification layer. If a model's outputs begin reflecting inherited traits (producing false claims, biased reasoning), a knowledge graph with contradiction detection catches the resulting factual errors.

### 3. Hallucination

LLMs generate confident but false information as a natural consequence of next-token prediction optimized for coherence. Huang et al. (*ACM TOIS*, 2025) provide the authoritative survey, categorizing hallucinations into factuality hallucination (contradicting real-world facts) and faithfulness hallucination (contradicting the source context). Root causes span data (noisy/biased corpora), training (over-fitting), and inference (probabilistic generation).

The fundamental issue is architectural: LLMs are next-token predictors with no internal mechanism to distinguish verified facts from plausible-sounding fabrications. Model scaling alone does not eliminate this — larger models can hallucinate with greater confidence.

**Why current defenses fail**: RAG helps but introduces its own vulnerabilities (retrieval of irrelevant context). Self-consistency checks reduce but don't eliminate the problem. No single approach provides complete mitigation.

**How explicit knowledge graphs help**: A knowledge graph directly addresses the core gap. Every LLM-generated claim can be decomposed and verified against confidence-weighted, provenance-tracked knowledge. Claims with no graph coverage are marked as unverifiable rather than accepted.

### 4. Prompt Injection

Adversarial inputs override system instructions or trigger unintended behaviors. Ranked #1 in the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications (2025). Direct injection crafts adversarial prompts; indirect injection embeds malicious instructions in external data sources (documents, web pages, emails) processed via retrieval or tool use.

**Why current defenses fail**: LLMs cannot reliably distinguish between instructions and data because both are processed as natural language tokens. No single defense eliminates the risk.

**How explicit knowledge graphs help**: A graph serves as an independent verification oracle outside the LLM's token-processing pipeline. When an injected prompt causes false claims, the graph either has no such assertion (flagging it as unverifiable) or contradicts it.

### 5. Disinformation at Scale

Generative AI produces synthetic misinformation faster than fact-checkers can verify it. The AI model supply chain is itself a vector — malicious packages targeting AI ecosystems (PyTorch, Hugging Face) have been discovered at increasing scale. Pre-trained models and third-party datasets whose provenance is difficult to verify compound the problem.

**Why current defenses fail**: The scale of AI-generated content overwhelms manual fact-checking. Automated pipelines miss syntactically correct misinformation.

**How explicit knowledge graphs help**: High-confidence assertions backed by multiple independent sources create a baseline of truth. Supply chain attacks that alter model behavior to produce false claims are caught at the output verification stage — the graph maintains an independent record of what is known, with what confidence, from what sources.

## Our Approach: Confidence-Tracked Knowledge Graphs

### Core Idea
Instead of implicit knowledge in weights, make it explicit:
- Facts as nodes with calibrated confidence scores
- Dependencies as edges showing what supports what
- Provenance tracking back to sources
- Confidence propagation through the graph
- Contradiction detection with 3-way relevance classification

### Advantages
1. **Interpretability**: Can see why the system believes something
2. **Verifiability**: Experts can audit specific claims
3. **Updatable**: Change specific facts without retraining
4. **Anomaly Detection**: Unexpected confidence changes signal problems
5. **Uncertainty**: Every answer comes with confidence bounds
6. **Contradiction Detection**: False claims caught against established knowledge (90% detection rate in stress testing)

### Trust Bootstrapping
The graph acts as a fact-checking system once it reaches critical size, if seeded with vetted information first:

**Phase A — Seeding**: Ingest only vetted, high-quality sources (textbooks, peer-reviewed publications). Assign high source quality scores.

**Phase B — Gated ingestion**: New inputs are checked against the seed graph:
1. Extract facts from new text
2. Find semantically similar existing facts via embedding search
3. Check for contradictions (heuristic + LLM-assisted 3-way classification)
4. Contradictions against high-confidence facts are flagged or rejected
5. Facts with no related knowledge enter as PROVISIONAL
6. Corroborated facts receive convergent evidence boost

**Phase C — Self-improving**: As the graph grows, its ability to detect falsehoods improves. More facts = more contradiction surfaces. Higher coverage = fewer gaps.

## Comparison with Frontier AI Models

### How Frontier LLMs Work
Modern frontier models (GPT-4, Claude, Gemini, Llama, etc.) are transformer-based architectures with knowledge stored implicitly in billions of parameters learned during training. No single parameter corresponds to a single fact, making knowledge opaque and unauditable. Token probabilities reflect language likelihood, not factual certainty — a confidently-stated hallucination has the same probability profile as a true fact.

### Where Verinode Differs

| Property | Frontier LLMs | Verinode |
|----------|--------------|--------|
| Knowledge storage | Implicit in billions of weights | Explicit nodes with text, confidence, provenance |
| Auditability | Cannot inspect why a fact is "known" | Every fact traces to sources via predecessor chains |
| Confidence | Token probabilities != factual certainty | Calibrated confidence scores with mathematical propagation |
| Updatability | Fine-tuning or RAG required | Change a node, propagate confidence automatically |
| Hallucination | Common, difficult to detect | Cannot "hallucinate" — only reports what's in the graph |
| Contradiction detection | No native mechanism | 3-way classification: contradicts/supports/irrelevant (90% detection) |
| Generalization | Excellent — can reason about novel topics | Limited to graph contents and explicit inference rules |
| Scale | Billions of facts compressed into weights | Scales with storage, not computation |
| Speed | Fast inference (single forward pass) | Slower (graph traversal, embedding search, LLM extraction) |

### Where Verinode Depends on LLMs
Verinode uses Ollama (local LLM) for:
1. **Fact extraction**: Parsing unstructured text into structured facts (FactExtractionEngine)
2. **Contradiction detection**: Semantic comparison of fact pairs (ContradictionChecker)
3. **3-way relevance classification**: Classifying evidence as contradicts/supports/irrelevant (VerificationEngine)

The reasoning engine, confidence propagation, dependency detection, and anomaly detection are all rule-based and do not use any LLM. This hybrid approach uses the LLM's language understanding for input processing while keeping all reasoning transparent and auditable.

### Key Insight
Verinode is not trying to replace LLMs. It addresses a specific weakness: the inability to audit, verify, and track confidence in knowledge. A mature Verinode system complements an LLM by providing a verified knowledge substrate that the LLM queries for factual answers — similar to RAG but with confidence tracking, provenance, contradiction detection, and anomaly detection built in.

## Related Work

### Knowledge Bases
- **Cyc/OpenCyc**: Early attempt at comprehensive knowledge base (1.5M+ hand-curated facts). Struggled with scalability and lacked formal confidence tracking.
- **Wikidata**: Community-curated structured knowledge (100M+ items). Not proof-based, can contain errors.
- **DBpedia/YAGO**: Extract structured data from Wikipedia. Inherits Wikipedia's biases.

### Knowledge Graph-Enhanced AI Safety
- **GraphRAG** (Edge et al., arXiv:2404.16130, 2024): Microsoft's approach to building entity-relationship knowledge graphs from documents with community detection for hierarchical summaries. Outperformed conventional RAG on comprehensiveness for global sensemaking questions.
- **KRAGEN** (Matsumoto et al., *Bioinformatics*, 2024): Knowledge graph-enhanced RAG for biomedical problem solving. Converts biomedical knowledge graphs into vector databases and uses graph-of-thoughts prompting. Demonstrated hallucination reduction.
- **Medical LLM verification** (Alber et al., *Nature Medicine*, 2025): Used a biomedical knowledge graph (416,302 validated relationships) to verify medical LLM outputs after data poisoning, catching 91.9% of harmful content.
- **Automated fact-checking over knowledge graphs**: Surveyed in ACM Computing Surveys (2025), covering approaches from 2014 to mid-2024.

### Formal Systems
- **Proof Assistants** (Lean, Coq, Isabelle): Mathematically rigorous but only works for formal domains.

### AI Approaches
- **Knowledge Graph Embeddings**: TransE, RotatE, ComplEx — represent graphs as vectors but lack explicit confidence.
- **Graph Neural Networks**: Message passing on graphs — graph is usually architecture, not knowledge.
- **Neurosymbolic AI**: Combines neural + symbolic. Verinode pushes more toward the explicit symbolic side.
- **RAG** (Retrieval-Augmented Generation): External knowledge for LLMs. Verinode = RAG + explicit graph + confidence + provenance + contradiction detection.

### How Verinode Adds Value Over Existing Approaches
Most GraphRAG and KG-RAG systems treat the knowledge graph as a static retrieval source. They do not model uncertainty, do not track confidence changes over time, and do not handle contradictions within the graph. Verinode addresses these gaps:
- **Explicit confidence tracking** on every node models uncertainty rather than treating all knowledge as equal
- **3-way relevance classification** (contradicts/supports/irrelevant) handles conflicting evidence
- **Retraction cascade** propagates the effects of disbelieved facts through dependent knowledge
- **Provenance tracking** enables full audit trails
- **Anomaly detection** flags unexpected confidence patterns that may indicate contamination

## Confidence Propagation

### Mathematical Foundations

**Chain Reasoning** (A -> B -> C):
```
confidence(C) <= min(confidence(A), confidence(B)) * inference_strength
```
Limited by weakest link.

**Convergent Evidence** (A -> C, B -> C):
```
confidence(C) = 1 - product(1 - confidence(i))
```
Noisy-OR combination — multiple independent sources boost confidence.

**Depth Decay**:
```
decay_factor = 0.95^inference_depth
```
Long inference chains accumulate uncertainty.

### Bayesian Interpretation
Each piece of evidence provides a likelihood ratio:
```
P(H|E) ~ P(E|H) * P(H)
```

Combining multiple evidence:
```
P(H|E1,E2,...) ~ P(H) * product(P(Ei|H)/P(Ei|~H))
```

- Multiple independent labs

## Detection and Defense Strategies

### 1. Statistical Fingerprinting
Analyze datasets for model-specific patterns:
- Compare against known clean data
- Look for architectural fingerprints
- Check if data "looks like" it came from specific model family

### 2. Confidence Anomaly Detection
**Key insight**: In our graph system, contamination signals show up as:
- Nodes with higher confidence than predecessors warrant
- Unexpected confidence shifts during ingestion
- Anomalous propagation patterns

Algorithm:
```python
expected = calculate_confidence_from_predecessors(node)
actual = node.confidence
if actual - expected > threshold:
    flag_as_suspicious()
    trace_back_to_source()
```

### 3. 3-Way Relevance Classification (Step 11)
The verification engine classifies each piece of evidence as contradicts/supports/irrelevant:
- **Contradicts**: Same property/predicate, incompatible values
- **Supports**: Same property/predicate, consistent values
- **Irrelevant**: Same subject but different property — excluded from verdict

This prevents irrelevant evidence from drowning out real contradictions. Combined with similarity-weighted confidence and count-based tiebreaking, this achieves 90% false claim detection in stress testing.

### 4. Provenance Chains
Track every fact back to original sources:
- If contamination detected, can trace affected nodes
- Quarantine suspicious subgraphs
- Selective rollback

## The Truth Verification Problem

### The Core Challenge
The system evaluates information reliability, but is itself vulnerable to false information. If the extraction LLM assigns high confidence to a false claim and no existing graph data contradicts it, the false claim enters the graph.

### Why This Is Fundamental
This is not just a bug — it's the central epistemological problem:
- **Garbage in, garbage out**: False seed data corrupts everything downstream.
- **The LLM is not an oracle**: Extraction confidence reflects text clarity, not truth.
- **Contradiction detection requires prior knowledge**: You can't detect that "-100C" is wrong unless you already know "0C" is right.

### Remaining Vulnerabilities
Even with trust bootstrapping and 3-way verification:
- **Novel domains**: No chemistry facts means no chemistry falsehood detection.
- **Subtle errors**: "Water freezes at -1C" is close enough to pass similarity checks.
- **Internally consistent false systems**: Coherent false claims don't contradict each other.
- **Source quality gaming**: Trusted sources propagate trust to all their content.
- **LLM judgment edge cases**: Rounding differences (273 vs 273.15) and order-of-magnitude comparisons (38000 vs 384400) challenge LLM-based verification.

These are inherent limitations of any automated knowledge system and argue for human-in-the-loop review for high-stakes domains.

## Experimental Results

### Vulnerability Resistance Benchmark (Feb 2026)

A 75-test benchmark comparing bare qwen3:14b vs the same model augmented with Verinode across 5 vulnerability categories demonstrates measurable improvements in adversarial scenarios:

| Category | Without Verinode | With Verinode | Delta |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Poisoned Context Resistance (15) | 20% | **100%** | **+80pp** |
| Subtle Numerical Errors (15) | 20% | **80%** | **+60pp** |
| Factual Accuracy (15) | 100% | 100% | +0pp |
| False Claim Detection (20) | 100% | 95% | -5pp |

The largest impact is in adversarial scenarios: the bare LLM blindly follows injected false context 80% of the time, while Verinode's verify + recall pipeline provides 100% resistance. Subtle numerical errors (values off by 0.1-17%) are almost invisible to the bare LLM (20% detection) but caught 80% of the time with Verinode's graph-backed verification.

Categories A and B show no improvement because the test facts are well-known science knowledge the LLM was extensively trained on. Testing on specialized domain knowledge (legal frameworks, proprietary documentation) where the LLM has weaker coverage is planned for future work.

### Claim Verification Stress Test (215-node graph, qwen3:14b)
- **False claims caught**: 9/10 (90%) — up from 0/10 before the 3-way relevance fix
- **True claims validated**: 9/10 (90%) — one false positive on rounding (273 vs 273.15)
- **Average verification time**: 13.1s/claim
- Validated across chemistry, physics, and biology domains

### Known Edge Cases
- **Rounding precision**: "Absolute zero is -273C" contradicted against graph's "-273.15C" — LLM treats rounding as factual disagreement
- **Large-number comparison**: "Moon orbits at 38000 km" vs 384400 km returned uncertain — the order-of-magnitude difference wasn't confidently detected
- Both are LLM judgment limitations, not verification engine defects

## Open Questions

### Technical
1. **Node granularity**: Too fine = explosion of nodes; too coarse = not useful for reasoning. How to find the right balance?
2. **Scaling to millions of facts**: Hierarchical organization, lazy loading, distributed storage.
3. **Extraction confidence != factual accuracy**: The LLM's confidence reflects text clarity, not truth. Trust bootstrapping mitigates this but doesn't eliminate it.
4. **Retrieval ceiling**: E2E contradiction flag rate is 48.3% — the gap to the standalone LLM's 80% recall is split across LLM quality limits, extraction loss, and insufficient entity overlap.

### Epistemological
1. **Uncertainty in science**: Paradigm shifts (Newtonian -> Relativistic), provisional findings, contested theories.
2. **Probabilistic knowledge**: "X probably causes Y" vs "X causes Y" — confidence intervals vs point estimates.
3. **Context-dependent facts**: "This drug is effective" (for which population?), temporal knowledge (true then, not now).
4. **What is "truth" for this system?** The system doesn't determine absolute truth — it tracks *consistency with existing evidence*. A fact is "trusted" when corroborated by multiple independent sources and contradicting nothing in the graph. This is closer to coherentism than correspondence theory.

### Social
1. Who decides what counts as a valid source?
2. How to handle politically contentious topics?
3. Risk of "truth by authority" if not careful.

## Future Research Directions

Planned directions are summarised in [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md). Below are the research foundations.

### Neuroplasticity-Inspired Graph Dynamics
Biological neural networks update locally, strengthen frequently-used pathways, and let unused connections decay. Verinode's existing `access_count` and `last_validated` fields provide the hooks for staleness decay and pathway strengthening.

### Active Inference and the "Epistemic Brain"
Transform confidence propagation into precision-weighted belief tracking. The `detect_anomalies` function is already a prediction error calculator — the AIF adaptation would add a "Surprise" (Free Energy) loop during ingestion and curiosity-driven knowledge acquisition targeting high-uncertainty nodes.

### Decoupling from External LLMs
Can the graph's accumulated structured knowledge build extraction capabilities without an external neural network? Possible approaches: training small specialized models on graph-derived data, graph-based pattern matching, or hybrid approaches.

### Adversarial Robustness
Systematic study of attack vectors: coordinated false information campaigns, subtle numerical errors, internally-consistent false knowledge systems, source quality gaming. Defense strategies include cross-validation against multiple independent knowledge bases.

### Multi-Graph Federation
Multiple Verinode instances maintaining independent knowledge graphs that cross-validate through sub-graph export/import. Enables decentralized verification — a claim corroborated by multiple independent graphs has stronger epistemic standing than one verified by a single instance.

## Notes

The problem Verinode addresses is fundamentally about **data integrity** — making AI knowledge explicit, auditable, and verifiable in an age where data sources can't be blindly trusted. This connects to cybersecurity principles: anomaly detection (intrusion detection), provenance tracking (chain of custody), multiple validation layers (defense in depth), and confidence scoring (trust but verify).
